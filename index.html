How it works

Ten nodes are scattered in a box and connected by a Delaunay triangulation. The two outermost nodes are pinned, and one of the rest is pulled straight down. The structure is solved with linear finite elements, so members turn red in compression and blue in tension as the load ramps on.

Displacements come out around half the size of the structure itself, which is why the result looks so dramatic — and why guessing it is harder than it first appears.

Scoring: 100% means you clicked exactly where the node settled. 0% means you missed by at least as far as the node travelled. The score is the fraction of its movement you predicted, not a distance in pixels.

v2 does not beat one line of arithmetic. It finds the loaded node almost perfectly from pixels — 0.97 correlation with its true position — and then drops it by roughly the average distance: 137px, where the truth averages 152px. That earns most of the score and none of the mechanics.

v3 does the mechanics. It reads the same screenshot, but instead of regressing an answer straight from pixels it recovers the structure — where the nodes are, which are supports, which pairs are joined — and then solves that structure by passing messages along the members, which is what an iterative solver does. Convolutions are the right tool for finding marks in an image and the wrong tool for a global solve; separating the two is the whole difference between 77% and 96%.

Its remaining error is almost entirely perception: a flawless solver reading node positions as precisely as v3 does would score about 98%.
